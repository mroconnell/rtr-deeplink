    What about Platform XYZ?

    Legistar, CivicPlus, PrimeGov, and CivicWeb aren't in the list above because none of them host video directly — they're calendar and agenda front-ends that hand the actual recording off to another platform. In practice: Legistar and CivicPlus meetings are almost always hosted on Granicus, while PrimeGov and CivicWeb meetings are almost always embedded straight from YouTube. So if your city runs Legistar, CivicPlus, PrimeGov, or CivicWeb, paste the meeting page link anyway — we follow it through to the real Granicus recording or YouTube embed underneath and transcribe it there. Destiny AgendaQuick and open.media work the same way — they're agenda front-ends rather than video hosts — but they're listed above rather than here, because a meeting archived from one of those pages can still be traced back to it. For a full breakdown of which detail-page platform, video platform, and transcript type each government uses, see the full jurisdiction detail table.

    What about YouTube?

    A lot of government agencies host their recordings on YouTube, which actually does a good job with transcripts and deep links already. If you're in a hurry working with a YouTube-embedded hearing, we encourage you to use YouTube's own built-in transcript and deep-link features — that's why YouTube isn't listed as its own platform above. Where a government page embeds or links a YouTube video (like the "Custom" platforms above), we'll happily read it for you; for a video pasted straight from youtube.com, YouTube's own tools are usually the faster path.

    Other projects worth knowing about

    For New York City Council meetings specifically, check out Vikram Oberoi's City Meetings NYC — a human-verified, diarized transcript project covering the NYC Council and the NYC Charter Revision Commission better than we could hope to. Oberoi's openness in sharing what he learned building it was fundamental to building this tool in the first place, so we're glad to send New York traffic his way.
